# Makespace storage terminal

This is a handheld device for scanning Makespace storage barcodes and
performing some operations based on them.

## Actions

 - scan a barcode and describe the thing it is on

 - scan a series of barcodes, of which the last is a location, to
   indicate that all the other items are now in that location

## Design

A handheld computer with wifi, with a barcode scanner, display, and
keypad (or touch screen), and a battery and wireless charging
receiver.

Design with touch screen (no keypad needed!)

Google says:

Many developers avoid native I2C for barcode scanners because
full-length barcode strings (like Code 128 or QR codes with dozens of
characters) require larger packet transfers than typical I2C register
reads. Using a hardware UART port on your MCU is usually much simpler
and less prone to buffer truncation than trying to shuttle long ASCII
strings over I2C.

Integrating a standard TTL/UART barcode scan engine with an ESP32
offers a major advantage: the ESP32 features three hardware UARTs
(serial ports), allowing you to bypass I2C entirely. You can connect
the scanner directly to one of the ESP32's free hardware serial ports,
leaving your primary hardware I2C pins entirely free for other sensors
or displays.
